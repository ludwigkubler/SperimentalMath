**Statement**:

> For any random 3-CNF formula F with n variables and m = cn clauses, let P_F be the Newton polytope of the polynomial p_F(x) = ∏_{C∈F} (1 - χ_C(x)), where χ_C is the indicator of the clause C. Then the expected number of edges of P_F is Ω(2^{δn}) for some δ > 0 depending on c, and this lower-bounds the expected number of nodes in any DPLL search tree for F up to polynomial factors: E[|T_DPLL(F)|] = Ω(edges(P_F) / poly(n)).

> The Newton polytope captures the combinatorial structure of cancellations and support interactions among clause indicators under multiplication, which corresponds to partial assignment pruning in DPLL. Its edge count reflects the number of adjacent monomial transitions during search, directly modeling variable decision paths.
