## 1. Conjecture
**Field A** (mathematical branch): Geometric Complexity Theory: dimension of the stabilizer Lie algebra g_p = {A ∈ Mat_n(Q) : Σ_i (A·x)_i ∂_i p(x) ≡ 0 as a polynomial} of a homogeneous polynomial p, computed by Gaussian elimination on the linear system extracted from the cubic-monomial coefficients; the canonical first-order Mulmuley–Sohoni / Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer obstruction used to separate det_n from perm_n via stabilizer dimension (2n²−1 vs. ~2n−2). Distinct from apolar/catalecticant rank and from Hessian rank.
**Field B** (complexity object): Lex-DPLL search-tree leaf count L_DPLL(F) on small UNSAT 3-CNF formulas at clause density α, equivalently tree-resolution leaf count up to constants — the canonical proof-complexity proxy for refutation hardness.

**Statement**:

> Define p_F ∈ Q[x_1,…,x_n] by p_F(x) = Σ_{clauses C} ε_C · x_{i_1}x_{i_2}x_{i_3}, where ε_C = (−1)^{#negated literals in C} and (i_1,i_2,i_3) are the indices of C's variables; let g_F be its GL_n stabilizer Lie algebra and dim_Q(g_F) its dimension. Conjecture: for every UNSAT 3-CNF F on n ≥ 8 variables, log_2 L_DPLL(F) + dim_Q(g_F) ≤ n + 2·⌈log_2 n⌉. A single UNSAT F violating this inequality (with positive margin) refutes it.

**Rationale (proposer's reasoning)**:

> GCT identifies high stabilizer dimension with maximally structured polynomials (the determinant's huge symmetry is exactly what trivializes its computation), and the orbit-closure containment program reads lower bounds off symmetry-budget mismatches. Translated to refutations, an UNSAT formula whose clause cubic carries large Lie symmetry should expose unit-propagation/decomposition structure, while symmetry-poor cubics correspond to the 'rigid' regime where DPLL is forced to branch combinatorially. Cubic clause polynomials are the cheapest GCT-style invariant capturing 3-SAT, and their stabilizer Lie algebras have not, to my knowledge, been linked to proof complexity.

        return 0

def gaussian_elimination(M):
    m, n = len(M), len(M[0])

                M[i][k] += factor * M[rank-1][k]
    return rank
