**Field B** (complexity object): Sum-of-Squares (Lasserre) refutation degree of Tseitin Max-CUT on expanders

**Statement**:

> For a d-regular graph G=(V,E) with odd parity σ:V→{0,1} (so the Tseitin formula T(G,σ) is unsatisfiable), define the degree-2 Tseitin moment polynomial P_{G,σ}(x)=Σ_{e,f∈E} c_{e,f}(σ)·x_e x_f with c_{e,f}(σ)=|{v∈e∩f: σ(v)=1}| if e,f share a vertex and 0 otherwise. Let δ(G,σ):=(number of strictly positive eigenvalues of the Hessian H_{P_{G,σ}}(1))−1 be the Lorentzian defect. Conjecture: for every d-regular Ramanujan expander G (λ₂(G)≤2√(d−1)) and every odd σ, δ(G,σ) ≥ ⌈α·|E|/d²⌉ for an absolute constant α≥1/8, and the resolution width w(T(G,σ)) (a known SOS-degree lower bound) satisfies w(T(G,σ)) ≥ 2+2δ(G,σ); a single (G,σ) violating either inequality refutes the conjecture.

**Rationale (proposer's reasoning)**:

> Lorentzian polynomials are precisely those whose Hessian has signature (1,m−1) at all positive points, a rigid algebraic property tied to Hodge-Riemann relations on matroids. Expansion forces the Tseitin parity obstruction to scatter across many edge-pairs, so the contribution matrix should have many positive eigendirections—a defect that should reappear as algebraic hardness in SOS, since SOS reductions preserve the moment-matrix signature up to bounded distortion. This connects Brändén–Huh signature combinatorics, which (to our knowledge) has not been used in proof complexity, to the open Tseitin/SOS lower-bound program.
