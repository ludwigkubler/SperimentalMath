### A.1 `e14f176e4ef1` — Tropical Self-Convolution Doubling Law (FALSIFIED, 2026-04-26)
**The headline output.** This is the *only* lean_verified entry of the system, the centrepiece of `papers/compendium_v02.tex`, and the surviving member of the "Tropical Fourier cluster" the May-8 audit hailed as "the principal scientific output."

**Statement (as recorded).** "For tropical polynomial f on Z_n with min-plus semiring, let g = TropicalConvolution(f, f). Then (i) MinimalFourierCoefficient(g) = 2·MinimalFourierCoefficient(f) up to additive error O(1/n) under the Maslov-dequantized TropicalFourierTransform, and (ii) DiscrepancyMeasure(g) ≤ 2·DiscrepancyMeasure(f). Complexity-corollary: distinguishing two tropical polynomials whose discrepancies differ by ε requires deterministic communication Ω(log(1/ε))."

**What the test actually computes.** Reading [test_code lines 9–48](notebook/2026-04.jsonl):
- `tropical_convolution(f, n)` = correct min-plus self-convolution on Z_n.
- `tropical_fourier_transform(f, β, n)` = correct Maslov-dequantized DFT: $F_β[f](k) = -(1/β) \log \sum_x e^{-β f(x)} e^{-2\pi i k x / n}$. Real and imaginary parts are split before logging (avoiding the bugs in the three retracted sister entries).
- `min_fourier_coeff` = min over k of |F_β[f](k)|.

So unlike `32a1e966ed26`, `44f82c29ed79`, `cca077d3c64c`, the TFT in this entry is genuinely a Fourier-type quantity, not a cyclic-rotation sum. **The implementation is fine.**

**What the result says.** Across 5 seeds × 180 instances each, `support_fraction = 0` everywhere. First counterexample at n=8, β=5: |MFC(g) − 2·MFC(f)| ≈ 3.785 vs the prescribed bound C/n = 5/8 = 0.625. The factor-6 gap is robust across seeds.

**My judgement: borderline-suggestive but not citable as-is.**

The reason is upstream of the test. The statement "MFC scales linearly under self-convolution with O(1/n) additive error" is not a known prediction of any literature on Maslov dequantization or tropical Fourier analysis. The rationale in the entry asserts that "Maslov dequantization predicts a clean linear-in-coefficient scaling," but this is a hand-wave: under Maslov dequantization $\beta \to \infty$, min-plus convolution becomes ordinary addition pointwise *only after rescaling by 1/β*; the TFT magnitude includes a $-1/β · \log$ which couples non-trivially to the convolution structure. There is no a-priori reason why MFC should compose additively under tropical self-convolution at finite β. So what the system has done is:

1. Invent a clean-looking quantitative conjecture without a derivation.
2. Run a Monte-Carlo at small n (n ∈ {8,16,32,64}) and small β (5,10,20).
3. Find that the clean conjecture is false at small n by a robust factor of ~6.
4. Call this a "refutation."

A genuine "refutation" in this area would refute a conjecture that someone in the tropical-geometry community would have proposed. This conjecture is proposed by no one; refuting it tells us nothing about what *should* hold.

**The Lean "refutation."** [`lean_verified/e14f176e4ef1/Eaudit.lean`](lean_verified/e14f176e4ef1/Eaudit.lean) declares `TropicalPolynomial := List Float`, ports the test code to Lean over `Float`, and at line 110–125 proves via `native_decide` that on the specific witness `[-7.0, 2.0, -7.0, -1.0, 2.0, -8.0, -10.0, -10.0]` with β=5, n=8, the inequality `|MFC(g) − 2·MFC(f)| > 0.625` holds in IEEE-754 double arithmetic. The README ([lean_verified/e14f176e4ef1/README.md, lines 14-17](lean_verified/e14f176e4ef1/README.md)) is honest: *"A proof over the real numbers. The Lean proof is over Float. A rigorous reformulation over ℝ would require interval arithmetic and is queued."* The Mathlib port (`e14f176e4ef1_mathlib_v0/Eaudit.lean` line 75) is a single `sorry` for the real-valued counterexample. So the certified Lean statement is *"on these eight IEEE-754 doubles, a specific Float computation produces a value > another specific value."* The empirical gap (3.78 vs 0.625) is wide enough that even the worst-case Float roundoff cannot close it, so morally the Lean theorem is correct, but as a publishable mathematical claim it certifies almost nothing.

**Compendium presentation.** The compendium's Lean *excerpt* (paper page 4) is even more misleading. The `myMetric` shown there
```
def myMetric (xs : List ℚ) : ℚ :=
  let f := xs.map (fun x => x)
  let g := f.map (· + 8)
  abs (g.sum - 2 * f.sum)
```
**does not call `tropicalConvolution`, `maslovTFTMagnitude`, or `minFC`.** It is just `|sum(xs+8) − 2·sum(xs)| = |8·len(xs) − sum(xs)|`. The proven theorem `myMetric witness ≠ myMetric (witness.map (·+1))` reduces to *"100 − 72 ≠ 108 − 88"*, i.e. 28 ≠ 20. That is true but has nothing to do with the conjecture. A reviewer at *Experimental Mathematics* who opened the Lean source from the paper would reject within five minutes; a reviewer who consulted only the `Eaudit.lean` file in `lean_verified/` would have a fair Float-level certificate of the numerical fact, but no certificate of the *meaning* of that fact.

**Verdict: artefact-prone. Re-do as follows.** If kept, the conjecture should be rewritten as "what *is* the rate?" The data (gap ≈ 3.78 at n=8, β=5) suggests the right scale is O(1) in n, not O(1/n). The next experiment should fit the actual scaling — that would be a publishable observation (rate measurement), not a refutation.

### A.2 `b0a4fb5d3039` — Forman-Ricci Min-Curvature × Monotone k-CLIQUE DNF (FALSIFIED, 2026-05-19)
**The second compendium-v02 entry. Only May `FALSIFIED`.**

**Statement.** A composite three-clause conjecture: (i) μ(F∧G) ≤ μ(F)+μ(G)+log₂(1+N) for arbitrary monotone DNFs; (ii) μ(F) ≤ 6c·log₂(1+N) when |F| ≤ N^c; (iii) μ(F*_v) ≥ v/4 for the canonical k-CLIQUE minterm DNF F*_v on K_v with k = ⌈log₂ v⌉.

**What "F*_v" actually is in the code.** [`generate_clique_dnf(v, k)`](notebook/2026-05.jsonl#L1-12) builds:
```python
for i in range(v):
    term = set()
    for j in range(i, v):
        if j != i:
            term.add((i, j))
    if len(term) >= k:
        terms.append(term)
```
This is **not** the k-CLIQUE minterm DNF. The minterms of the k-CLIQUE indicator on $K_v$ are the $\binom{v}{k}$ sets of $\binom{k}{2}$ edges, one per k-clique. The code builds for vertex i the set of edges $\{(i,j) : j>i\}$ — i.e. the **out-star** at vertex i — and keeps it only if it has size ≥ k. For v=4, k=⌈log₂ 4⌉=2:
- i=0: {(0,1),(0,2),(0,3)} — size 3 ≥ 2 ✓
- i=1: {(1,2),(1,3)} — size 2 ≥ 2 ✓
- i=2: {(2,3)} — size 1 < 2 ✗
- i=3: ∅ — size 0 < 2 ✗

Output: two edge-disjoint stars. The overlap graph has zero edges; by definition μ=0. Conjecture (iii) requires μ ≥ v/4 = 1. So 0 < 1 → "falsified."

**My judgement: bogus.** The "F*_v" in the code is not the F*_v in the statement. So the refutation refutes a claim about an object that the test does not construct. Even if it were correctly built, v=4, k=2 produces a 4-vertex graph where "k-clique" is "edge" — the conjecture's asymptotic content (clique on log₂ v vertices in K_v with v large) is not probed.

**The Lean refutation.** [`lean_counterexamples/b0a4fb5d3039.lean`](papers/compendium_v02.tex#L147-197) just records the v=4 case literally: it asserts `T₀ ∩ T₁ = ∅`, hence μ=0, hence 0 < v/4 = 1. The Lean is *correct given the code's interpretation*. But the code's interpretation is wrong.

A reviewer at any combinatorics venue would catch this in one read. **Not citable.**

**What might be salvageable.** The underlying intuition — that Forman-Ricci curvature on a term-overlap graph captures "sunflower core" density and so should saturate on cliques — is a legitimate research direction. But it needs (a) correct DNF construction, (b) testing on real k-CLIQUE on v ∈ {10, 20, 40} (the only regime where "k = ⌈log₂ v⌉" stops being trivial), and (c) a clear story for why a *graph* invariant on the term-overlap graph (which throws away which Boolean variables appear) should track the Boolean complexity of the DNF.

### A.3 `15ae8fd62af0` — Grothendieck–Witt × Resolution Width (SUPPORTED, demoted)
**Caught by April audit; demoted to INCONCLUSIVE pending reformulation.**

**Statement.** $Q_φ(x) = \sum_{c∈φ} ⟨c,x⟩^2 \mod 2$. The 2-rank of the polarisation $B(x,y) = Q_φ(x+y) - Q_φ(x) - Q_φ(y)$ equals resolution width up to additive 3.

**The bug.** Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, $a^2 = a$, so $Q_φ(x) = \sum_c ⟨c,x⟩$ is *linear*. Therefore $B \equiv 0$ identically, and the polarisation has 2-rank 0 for every formula. The conjecture as written says width(φ) ≤ 3 for every CNF formula — a trivially false statement at any n ≥ 5 with an unsatisfiable instance of width ≥ 4 (which exist already at n=5 for Tseitin formulas).

**What the code did.** It silently reinterpreted $⟨c,x⟩$ as an *integer* inner product, squared, then reduced mod 2 — a different object. The April audit caught this. The system is honest: the demotion is recorded in `retractions.json` and the entry stayed out of `supported_findings.md`.

**My judgement: an honest demotion of an algebraic confusion.** The audit was correct. The "salvageable intuition" claim in the audit document is generous: $a^2 = a$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ is undergraduate; any pipeline that lets a quadratic-form-over-$\mathbb{F}_2$ conjecture through without flagging this lacks a basic algebra check.

### A.4 `84f371b65a13` — Toric Degeneration Depth × Decision Tree Complexity (FALSIFIED, 2026-04-23)
**Statement.** D(f) = Θ(d(φ)), where d(φ) is the minimum number of Gröbner degenerations taking $P_φ$ into a toric variety.

**Counterexample reported.** n=4, clauses=`((0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,2),(1,3),(3,3))`, D(f)=1, d(φ)=3.

**Two problems.**
1. The "clauses" are 2-tuples of variable indices, not literals with signs. So this is not a CNF formula in the standard sense; it's a hypergraph. The conjecture as stated is about CNF.
2. The "clause" `(3,3)` is a degenerate hyperedge with a repeated vertex. Under any reasonable reading it's either ignored or it forces variable 3 in some way.

**More importantly, what is "d(φ)"?** The test computes some number labelled `d(phi)` but the entry's test code (not shown above to save space) computes it via a brute-force enumeration over small term orders on a polytope built from the satisfying assignments. The conjecture *as written* requires "Gröbner degenerations producing a toric variety with monomial ideal matching the clause structure"; the test's d is just a count of distinct term orders that produce distinct initial ideals. These are different objects.

**My judgement: artefact.** A real instance of d(φ) ≠ D(f) requires d defined precisely and shown to be well-defined. The reported counterexample on a CNF that includes a degenerate clause is not a refutation, it is a category error.

### A.5 `fe46162e441f` — Euler Characteristic of Directed Flag Complex = DPLL Backtrack Count (FALSIFIED, 2026-04-24)
**Statement.** χ(Δ_dir(φ)) = B(φ) for every 3-CNF on n ≤ 20.

**Counterexample.** n=3, m=12, clauses listed in the stdout: `[(3,-1,-2),(1,-3,-2),(-1,-3,2),(-1,-2,3),(-2,3,-1),(1,2,3),(1,-2,3),(-3,-2,-1),(-2,1,3),(2,3,-1),(-3,-2,-1),(-2,3,-1)]`. χ_dir = −1, B = 4.

**Look at the clauses.** With n=3 variables there are exactly $2^3 \cdot \binom{3}{3} \cdot 2^0 = 8$ distinct 3-clauses (choose 3 distinct vars, choose signs; over 3 vars all 3-clauses contain all 3 vars). The list above has 12 clauses, which means **some are repeated**: indeed `(-3,-2,-1)` appears twice (positions 8 and 11), and `(-2,3,-1)` appears twice (positions 5 and 12, modulo ordering). The "formula" is not a set of distinct clauses but a multiset.

The conjecture is stated for "every 3-CNF formula," which is usually interpreted as a set of distinct clauses. With repeats, both the directed flag complex and the DPLL trace become ill-defined (does the DPLL solver see duplicates? do duplicates count as different generators of the flag complex?). The "counterexample" is at n=3, m=12 on a multi-set, which is a degenerate corner.

A stronger objection: the statement says "*equals* B(φ)" for a *fixed* DPLL solver with MOMS heuristic. Two arbitrary integer-valued invariants on tiny instances need not be equal; the conjecture is essentially saying "two random small integers should be equal," which is trivially false. A statement of this shape has no business being on the conjecture list.

**My judgement: trivial refutation of a non-conjecture.** Not citable.

### A.6 `ad27563e9b62` — Clifford Signature × Resolution Width (FALSIFIED, 2026-04-25)
**Statement.** $|w(φ) − |\text{sig}_\pm(Q_φ)|| \le 2$.

**The data.** Reading the stdout tail:
- n=5, m∈{8,9,10}: w=6, sig=0, gap = 6
- n=6, m∈{7,…,11}: w=7, sig=0, gap = 7
- n=6, m=12: w=7, sig=−1, gap = 6
- "Counterexample": n=3, m=3, w=3, sig=0, gap = 3

For n=5 the worst possible resolution width is 5 (the number of variables); reporting w=6 already indicates a bug in the width computation. More fundamentally, **the test never produces sig ≠ 0 except in one tiny corner**: the symmetric matrix of pairwise products of 2-clause indicators over $\mathbb{F}_2$ lifted to $\mathbb{R}$ is, by construction, sparse, and on the n ≤ 6 instances the system sampled, its real-valued signature is generically zero.

**Why this is structural.** "Lifting" an $\mathbb{F}_2$ matrix to $\mathbb{R}$ is ambiguous (0,1-lift? signed-lift?) and the test uses the 0,1-lift. A 0,1 symmetric matrix has signature pattern determined by its eigenvalues, which for small sparse matrices tend to cluster near 0 with one or two large eigenvalues — so sig ≈ 0 generically. Meanwhile w is the algorithmic resolution width, which on random unsatisfiable 3-CNFs scales like n (Ben-Sasson–Wigderson 2001). So the two quantities track different things; reporting "they are not equal" is reporting noise.

**My judgement: trivial. Not a refutation of anything that was ever plausibly true.**

### A.9 `025337d8bbcc` — SOS Refutation Rounds = Symmetric Rank(A) (FALSIFIED, 2026-04-26)
**Statement.** For random 3-SAT with m = 1.5n, SOS round complexity = Θ(rank_sym(A)) where A is clause-variable incidence.

**Counterexamples.** rank(A)=14, SOS=3; rank(A)=11, SOS=4; rank(A)=5, SOS=4; etc.

**The trouble.** Two issues:
1. m = 1.5n is *below* the SAT threshold (≈4.267); random 3-CNFs there are almost surely SAT. SOS round complexity for refuting an unsatisfiable formula is undefined when the formula is satisfiable. The test must be either (a) returning a default value for SAT formulas, or (b) computing some other quantity it calls "SOS rounds" — but at m=1.5n almost no random formula is UNSAT.
2. "rank_sym(A)" of the clause-variable incidence matrix is bounded by min(m, n) = 1.5n at most, but actually closer to n (since incidence matrices are typically full rank). So the conjecture would say SOS rounds ≈ n on random 3-SAT — which is at the SOS Ω(n) lower bound (Grigoriev 2001, Schoenebeck 2008). So the conjecture is *roughly* what's known, but with `=` instead of `≥`.

A refutation of "SOS rounds = rank" via "rank=14, SOS=3" tells us nothing because both quantities are misdefined for the regime.

**My judgement: refutes a malformed quantity. Not informative.**

### Recurring failure patterns (beyond the four 2026-05-08 retractions)
Reading 30+ tests gives me five additional recurring failure patterns the May-8 audit did not call out:

1. **Conjectures use system-invented terms whose only definition is in the test code.** "Induced Kolmogorov flow," "mixer profile," "Maslov-dequantized TFT" (the system's variant), "tropical proof rank," "phase cells" — these are not literature objects. The "refutation" of a conjecture about a system-defined object reduces to the test code's behaviour. There is no external falsifiability.

2. **Conjectures of shape "two integer-valued invariants are equal on small instances."** `χ_dir = B(φ)`, `D(f) = d(φ)`, `rank_sym = SOS_rounds`, `χ = sig`. Two distinct integer invariants are almost always unequal on random small instances. Refuting "they are equal" is a tautology. The conjecture should be "≤", "≥", or "Θ(f(n))" — not "=".

3. **Counterexamples on n ≤ 5 for asymptotic claims.** Resolution width on n=3,4 is bounded by 3,4 by definition; "refuting" anything involving width on n=3 is degenerate. The May-8 audit applied this lesson to the Khovanov retraction but not consistently.

4. **Conditional conjectures whose hypothesis is not verified.** `98ce17e2db79` conditions on "proof size = Θ(n)" but never measures proof size. `385e517e003a` conditions on "hard on average" but never measures hardness. The test samples random instances assuming the antecedent.

5. **Verdict logic parses pattern-matched stdout, not exit code or assertion outcomes.** `98ce17e2db79` produced a Python traceback and was still labelled FALSIFIED. `8f4860266324` emitted the string `"mapping_undefined"` (an internal failure) and the verdict parser took this as a counterexample value.

### The compendium itself
`papers/compendium_v02.tex` contains exactly **2 refutations**. The body text framings:
> "*the primary positive contribution of the SEC P-vs-NP research program: not a resolution of P vs NP, but a continuously-growing, fully-verified catalogue of what does not work.*"

is undermined by both refutations failing audit. The Discussion paragraph lists Algebraic / Tropical / Topological themes "consistently refuted by tautological dispatch patterns or hardcoded invariants" — but those very patterns are still in the compendium's two surviving entries.

**My recommendation on the compendium: it is not currently submittable.** A reviewer at *Experimental Mathematics* or *Discrete Mathematics* would reject in the first round on grounds that (a) the Lean proofs do not formalize the stated conjectures, (b) one of the two main objects (F*_v in entry 2) is not what the code builds, and (c) the conjectures themselves come from no identified literature predecessor. The right move is **hold the compendium and replace it with a `negative_observations.md` document at v0.1 status** that frames these as "the autonomous engine produced 16 falsification candidates; after human audit, none are robust enough to be a literature contribution; the rate of artefacts indicates pipeline issues documented in MULTIAGENT_PIPELINE.md."

## Parte D — Raccomandazioni

### Filoni che meritano deepening con review umana
1. **Tropical discrepancy at finite β.** Drop the "refutation" framing on `e14f176e4ef1` and instead measure the actual scaling: $|MFC(g) − 2·MFC(f)|$ as a function of n at fixed β, and as a function of β at fixed n. If the empirical scaling is $O(\log n)$ or $O(1)$ universally, that is a *positive observation* worth a 4-page note. Pair with a human collaborator who knows tropical analysis (Maclagan-Sturmfels) and Maslov dequantization (Mikhalkin, Litvinov).
2. **Forman-Ricci on hypergraphs of real Boolean functions.** Fix the `generate_clique_dnf` bug (build genuine k-clique minterm DNFs, not out-stars). Test on v ∈ {10, 16, 20}. Even producing a *correct* table of μ(F*_v) vs v would be a starting datum.
3. **CG-KW as a programme, not a productive framework.** The CGKW.lean file is a useful *type-level statement* of A1–A5. Treat it as a research roadmap, not as something to test on cycles. Tests of A1 on Andreev or IND need explicit, well-defined `κ(f)` computations — which the system has not implemented (`κ` is `opaque` in the Lean).

### Filoni da abbandonare
4. **Any conjecture of shape "two integer invariants are equal."** Reject at the planner stage. Demand `≤`, `≥`, or `Θ(f(n))`.
5. **Anything involving "tropical Fourier transform" of the system's homebrew definition.** Until a precise definition referencing literature exists (e.g., Litvinov–Maslov 2007), this is a dead end.
6. **Anything involving "Kolmogorov flow on dynamical circuits."** The compound has no literature meaning. The Ergodic Circuit Framework's primitives are unimplemented opaque types.
7. **Anything tested at n ≤ 5 only.** Resolution width, decision tree depth, etc., have trivial closed-form bounds at n ≤ 5. Counterexamples at n ≤ 5 should be rejected.

### Pipeline fixes (beyond TARGET_ARCHITECTURE.md)
1. **Critic must read code AND check that the code computes the statement.** The May-8 audit's §4.5 already requires this; verify the next 30 cycles' critic outputs include a paragraph titled *"statement-vs-test divergence audit"* and that it actually engages with the algebra.
2. **Verdict parser must distinguish "test crashed" from "test produced counterexample."** Exit code != 0 → INCONCLUSIVE, never FALSIFIED. Stdout containing Python tracebacks → INCONCLUSIVE. Counterexample equal to a placeholder string like `"mapping_undefined"` → INCONCLUSIVE.
3. **Reject cycles at the planner stage where the conjecture's hypothesis cannot be empirically verified.** "Hard on average," "resolution proof size is Θ(n)" — if the test doesn't measure the antecedent, reject before running.
4. **Asymptotic gate.** A conjecture about *every n*, refuted on n ≤ 5, is not a refutation but a triviality. Refutations require either (a) refutation regime n ≥ 16, or (b) proof that the n ≤ 5 case is *not* degenerate.
5. **Demote SCOOPED to "TOPICALLY_ADJACENT" when the LLM's reasoning says "the papers do not directly address but are related."** Reserve SCOOPED for the cases where a specific paper proves or refutes the specific statement.
6. **Lean gate must formalize the *statement*, not a downsampled cousin.** The compendium-paper Lean excerpt for `e14f176e4ef1` (which proves `sum(xs+8) ≠ sum(xs+1+8)`, unrelated to the tropical conjecture) is the smoking gun. The Lean theorem text must reference the same primitives as the Python statement, even if the proof uses Float computation.
7. **Stop generating "Field A × Field B" cross-products as a planning move.** This generates a continuous river of "vocabulary stapled to objects." Replace with: "Pick one of the six live frameworks; advance one of its axioms by one inferential step." This is what mathematicians do.
8. **Track one programme for ≥ 50 sequential cycles.** Current behaviour: ~1 cycle per (Field A, Field B) pair, then move on. No deepening possible. The CG-KW programme has the right shape but isn't being executed: its A1 has been touched in 1–2 cycles and dropped.

### What would make the next compendium credible
A `compendium_v03.tex` that I would consider submitting would contain:
- One framework (say tropical-discrepancy or Forman-Ricci-on-Boolean-DNFs), studied across ≥ 50 cycles.

The system is *not* failing because of model quality. The autoformalizer, critic, judge, and skeptic gates are functioning as designed. The system is failing because:

1. **The conjecture generator overproduces and never deepens.** 1772 cycles, ~1700 unique (FieldA, FieldB) pairs. Mathematics is the opposite of this distribution.

The audit of 2026-05-08 was a real act of scientific integrity. The retractions are correct. The cheapest fix is to constrain the planner to one framework at a time for 50+ cycles before allowing it to switch, and to put a human (you) at the front of the funnel: each morning, pick the one or two conjectures most worth a cycle, kill the rest.
