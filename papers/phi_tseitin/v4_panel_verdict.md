### Lens: statistical

I have enough context to analyze the draft on its own terms. Let me now provide a focused statistical review.

Verdict: REFUTED

Strongest concerns:

- **Single-point claim of "2.56x default" at n=60 no-restart is not a finding, it is one cell.** The headline "Disabling restart adds +0.323 to the log-log slope" is driven almost entirely by the n=60 row (9.38e8 vs 3.66e8). The +0.323 cannot be distinguished from "n=60 happened to draw a hard seed set" without a paired-by-seed bootstrap CI on the slope *difference* — which the draft explicitly admits is not computed (§11 item 5). The "≈10% of the gap is restart" headline is therefore not statistically supported.

- **The combined-window slope 4.43 is arithmetically inconsistent with the components and is driven by censored data.** The draft reports B.1 alone (n=14–30) at 4.256 and A.1 alone (n=32–36) at 8.667, then "combined" at 4.433. But A.1's own three-point slope of 8.667 is computed on a window where the rightmost point (n=36) is downward-biased by 30% censoring; the draft acknowledges this biases the *combined* slope downward, yet the combined slope (4.43) is *higher* than the uncensored B.1 leg (4.26). Reporting 4.43 as *the* headline while admitting it is biased downward and lacks a CI is selective reporting.

- **The Esteban–Torán + ADRNV Lemma 12 chain in Corollary C2 has an undefended step.** ADRNV Lemma 12 gives cumulative-space Ω(s²) where s is *maximum* clause-space, not minimum. Esteban–Torán gives clause-space = Ω(n) for Tseitin on expanders, where clause-space is the *minimum* over refutations. To compose, you need the *minimum* clause-space lower bound to imply a lower bound on the *maximum* clause-space along the same trace that minimises cumulative space — these are not the same minimiser. The chain ClSp_min ≥ n ⇒ MaxSp_along_min_cumulative_trace ≥ n is not obviously valid; the draft asserts it without citation to a specific ADRNV proposition that bridges minimisers. The "unconditional" Ω(n²) in C2 is therefore overstated even granting Lemma A (ii).

- **Lemma A part (ii) inequality direction is fine, but the "C2 is unconditional on heuristic" claim contradicts the per-trace identification.** The draft says C2 holds "unconditionally on the heuristic, but conditional on the ADRNV identification of Lemma A (ii)". But Lemma A (ii) is a *formula-level* inequality Φ_count^{DP,h}(F) ≥ CSpace_cum(F) that holds by the min-over-traces argument alone — it does not require any ADRNV-Lean bridge for the inequality direction, only for the *semantic identification* used downstream. The labelling of what is and is not conditional is muddled: the lower bound c·n² on CSpace_cum is conditional on Esteban–Torán + Lemma 12 (a complexity-theoretic fact, not a Lean port), while the Φ ≥ CSpace_cum step is the tautological one. Inverting these in the prose obscures where the real burden lies.

With clustered/paired data the effective n is smaller than 8, and the p-value is anti-conservative. More importantly, the tie at n=18 is excluded from the test on the grounds of "suspected seed contamination" — but the contamination is undiagnosed. Excluding a tie *and* claiming the exclusion strengthens the alternative is double counting: if seeds are contaminated at n=18, they may be contaminated elsewhere, and the cleaner test is the two-sided sign on 8 trials (7-0-1) treating the tie as 0.5 each side, which still gives a small p but the honest figure, not the post-hoc p=0.0078.

The "+0.323 slope contribution from `--restart=false`" is computed without a CI on a 9-point series where the entire effect is concentrated in the last point (n=60), with five interior points showing zero effect; the "≈10% of the DRAT/DP gap is restart" headline is a single-point inference dressed as a slope decomposition. ### Lens: theoretical

I'll proceed with the adversarial review based on the draft text itself.

Verdict: REFUTED

Strongest concerns:

- **Corollary C2's chain is broken / mis-cites ADRNV Lemma 12.** The paper claims "ADRNV Lemma 12 (p. 38:13): maximal space s implies cumulative-space Ω(s²)." This is the wrong direction of an obvious inequality. Trivially CSpace_cum(π) = Σ_t |M_t| ≥ max_t |M_t| = MaxSpace(π), but the deeper ADRNV result needed to upgrade Esteban–Torán's *linear* clause-space LB to a *quadratic* cumulative-space LB requires a length-times-space style argument (their Theorem ~13 / Cor 14, the "s² over n" type bound), not Lemma 12 alone. If Lemma 12 really did give CSpace_cum ≥ MaxSpace², ADRNV's open problem on Tseitin (p. 38:19) would already be closed by Esteban–Torán — yet the paper itself cites that exact open problem as still open. This is an internal contradiction: either C2 is right and ADRNV's open problem is solved (it isn't), or C2 mis-cites Lemma 12 and the Ω(n²) anchor evaporates.

- **Lemma A part (ii) "inequality direction" is the wrong sign for an upper witness.** The paper writes Φ_count(DP,h)(F) ≥ CSpace_cum(F), and then says every empirical exponent is an *upper witness* for the asymptotic exponent of CSpace_cum. But an upper bound on CSpace_cum gives **no information about its growth rate** — measuring α=4.43 on Φ is consistent with CSpace_cum ∈ Θ(n²), Θ(n^3.5), or Θ(n^4.43). Worse, in §9 the paper writes "Φ_count(DP,min-occ)(F_n) ≥ CSpace_cum(F_n) ≥ c·n²" and then says α≈4.43 "is consistent with — and is an upper witness for — this lower bound." An exponent on the upper side of an inequality is *not* a witness (upper or lower) for the lower side's growth rate; the logical content is null. C1's statement "if Φ = Θ(n^α) then CSpace_cum = O(n^α)" is also wrong — Φ ≥ CSpace_cum gives CSpace_cum ≤ Φ pointwise, hence at most O(n^α), but only if Φ = O(n^α); the Θ assumption is unnecessary and misleading.

- **Esteban–Torán is mis-applied to random 3-regular Tseitin.** Esteban–Torán requires an *expander* underlying graph with an odd-charge function. The B.1/A.1 headline corpus is *plain* (uncertified) random 3-regular Tseitin, on which expansion is not certified — only the §7.2 girth-5 corpus is. The Ω(n²) anchor in §9 therefore does not transfer to the 4.43 headline family; the paper conflates the certified-expander sub-corpus (where the LB applies) with the rand_3reg headline corpus (where it doesn't). Random 3-regular graphs are *whp* expanders, but "whp" is not the same as a per-instance certificate, and the §6.1 grid for the headline family explicitly carries the "uncertified" label.

- **The "--simplify=false ≈ no inprocessing" claim is solver-semantically wrong.** In kissat, `--simplify=0` disables top-level *preprocessing/inprocessing scheduling*, but variable elimination, vivification, subsumption, probing, etc. are individually gated by their own options (`--eliminate`, `--vivify`, `--probe`, `--substitute`, ...). The paper's flag matrix tests `--simplify=false` and `--eliminate=false` separately and concludes inprocessing is "statistically null," but it never disables vivification, probing, or substitution. The "intrinsic CDCL" 90% residual is therefore not isolated from inprocessing — only from the two inprocessing knobs that happen to have been toggled. The mechanism claim that restart contributes ~10% and CDCL the remaining ~90% is unsupported.

- **The n=60 "2.56× default" claim rests on a single n-point with no CI and is presented as a slope-contributing effect.** Table in §10 shows restart-effect 1.00 through n=26, then 1.12, 1.22, 1.01, 2.56 at n=30,40,50,60 — non-monotone, with the 1.01 at n=50 essentially null. Drawing "+0.323 slope contribution" from a non-monotone sequence dominated by one tail point (n=60) is exactly the kind of single-point inference the paper criticises elsewhere (it withdrew 2- and 3-point slope CIs in §6.6). The paired-by-seed bootstrap CI for the +0.323 is not reported, and §11 item 5 admits the headline DRAT−DP +3.28±0.4 is "preliminary, not a fully bootstrapped CI." That admission is fatal for any "mechanism" claim in §10.

Refutation: The paper's theoretical spine — Lemma A → Corollary C2 → Proposition in §9 → "upper witness" framing — is logically incoherent in the direction it is used. C2 mis-cites ADRNV Lemma 12 to manufacture an Ω(n²) lower bound that, if it actually existed in the form stated, would have closed ADRNV's own p.38:19 open problem on Tseitin via Esteban–Torán; that internal contradiction alone is sufficient grounds for an arXiv reviewer to reject. Compounding this, the inequality Φ ≥ CSpace_cum is repeatedly described as making empirical Φ-exponents "upper witnesses" for CSpace_cum's growth rate, which is a category error: upper bounds on a quantity tell you nothing about its asymptotic exponent, and C1's formal phrasing ("Φ = Θ(n^α) implies CSpace_cum = O(n^α)") is both unnecessary and informally wrong as a "witness" statement. The Esteban–Torán anchor is then applied to the uncertified rand_3reg headline corpus, where the per-instance expansion needed by the theorem is not certified. On the implementation side, `--simplify=false` in kissat does not disable all inprocessing (vivification, probing, substitution remain on by default), so the mechanism claim that splits the DRAT/DP gap into "10% restart + 90% intrinsic CDCL" is not isolated. The n=60 2.56× ratio is one non-monotone tail point in a five-point sequence, and the headline +3.28±0.4 and +0.323 numbers are explicitly admitted in §11 to lack proper bootstrap CIs. The harness-fidelity claim is also weak: the Lean `cumulativeEntropy` matches a list-of-states sum trivially, but no verification is shown that the Python DP harness's `activeClauses` sequence matches the Lean `ProofState.activeClauses` sequence step-for-step (no oracle test, no cross-check on small instances). MAX_DB = 1.5×10⁶ on the headline corpus and the resolvent-product bound `len(pos)·len(neg) > 8·MAX_DB` interact with `min-occ` in a way that left-censors precisely the hardest instances, biasing the slope downward in a way that *strengthens* the headline number (the true slope is higher than 4.43, making the drift problem worse, not better — the paper acknowledges direction but not that this undermines the "no asymptotic stabilisation" framing as evidence rather than artifact).

What would close the verdict:
1. Correct the C2 citation: either cite the specific ADRNV theorem (likely their length×space or s²/n result, not Lemma 12) that actually upgrades Ω(s) clause-space to Ω(s²) cumulative-space on Tseitin, *or* concede that no such unconditional Ω(n²) anchor exists for the rand_3reg headline family and remove the "upper witness for Ω(n²)" framing from §9 and the abstract.

3. Restrict the Esteban–Torán anchor to the certified-expander §7.2 corpus and reframe the rand_3reg headline as the family where the anchor does *not* formally apply.
