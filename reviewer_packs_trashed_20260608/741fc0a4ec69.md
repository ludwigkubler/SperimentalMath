## 1. Conjecture

**Field B** (complexity object): Resolution refutation length of Tseitin formulas T(G,σ)

**Statement**:

> Let G=(V,E) be any connected graph (possibly irregular) and B_G be its 2|E|×2|E| Hashimoto non-backtracking operator (B_{(a,b),(c,d)}=1 iff b=c and a≠d). Define the Ihara pole gap ν(G):= log|λ_1(B_G)| − log max{|λ|: λ∈Spec(B_G), λ∉{±λ_1,±1}}, set to 0 if no such second eigenvalue exists. Then for every odd-charge labeling σ:V→{0,1} with Σσ(v)≡1 (mod 2), the minimum Resolution refutation length L(T(G,σ)) satisfies log_2 L(T(G,σ)) ≥ c·|V|·ν(G) for an absolute constant c>0, and ν(G)=O(1/|V|) on every graph admitting an O(1)-vertex separator.

**Rationale (proposer's reasoning)**:

> Standard expansion arguments use λ_2 of the adjacency or Laplacian matrix, but the Bass–Hashimoto operator captures non-backtracking closed-walk multiplicities that better reflect how Resolution must trace clause-derivation paths without revisiting variables. Because B_G is the natural transfer operator of the Ihara zeta function, its second-pole gap is the right quantitative analogue of a ‘prime number theorem’ for graphs — and bottleneck graphs (long paths, dumbbells, weak cuts) automatically have collapsed gap. Importing zeta-function multiplicities into proof complexity is essentially uncharted (<5 papers) and avoids the algebrization barrier because B_G is a combinatorial integer matrix derived purely from the formula’s underlying graph.

    V, E = graph
