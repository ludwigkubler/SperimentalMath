## 1. Conjecture
**Field A** (mathematical branch): p-adic carry combinatorics: Kummer's theorem on the 2-adic valuation of binomial coefficients via base-2 carry counts, lifted to the 'carries polynomial' C_n(x,y) = sum over (i,j) of [carry occurs at bit position k when adding i and j in binary] x^i y^j; an elementary number-theoretic gadget rarely deployed in proof complexity
**Field B** (complexity object): Extended Frege (EF) proof complexity: number of distinct lines (sub-formulas after eliminating extension axioms) in a tree-like EF refutation of the pigeonhole principle PHP_n^{n+1} encoded as a CNF

**Statement**:

> Let kappa(n) be the GF(2)-rank of the n x n matrix M_n with M_n[i,j] = parity of the number of binary carries when adding i and j (0<=i,j<n). The conjecture: for every tree-like EF refutation Pi of PHP_n^{n+1}, the number of distinct sub-formulas appearing in Pi (the 'EF line count') is at least kappa(n) + n, and asymptotically kappa(n) = n - O(log n) so this gives a near-linear unconditional EF line lower bound. A single PHP_n^{n+1} (n<=12) admitting a tree-like EF derivation with fewer than kappa(n) + n distinct lines refutes the conjecture.

**Rationale (proposer's reasoning)**:

> Kummer carries control 2-adic structure of binomial coefficients, which in turn govern the counting identities a Frege system must internalize to derive PHP; the carries-matrix rank is a clean, computable surrogate for that structural obstruction. Linking carry-rank to EF line count proposes that the same arithmetic obstruction that makes binomial 2-adic valuations dense also forces many distinct extension formulas. This bridge is computationally tractable (just bit-parity and GF(2) Gaussian elimination) and has, to my knowledge, never been formulated.
