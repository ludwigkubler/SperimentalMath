**Field B** (complexity object): Output linear (parity) bias of the Nisan-Wigderson generator NW_{D,f}: {0,1}^d -> {0,1}^m against parity distinguishers chi_T, T subset of [m], built from a hard function f:{0,1}^l -> {0,1}

**Statement**:

> Let D=(S_1,...,S_m) be an (l,a)-NW design over [d] with a<=log2(m), and let P_D be the poset on nonempty subsets of [m] ordered by T<=T' iff the symmetric-difference indicator of (union of S_i for i in T) is a 2-adic refinement of that of T'. Define the Walsh antichain width W(D)=max over antichains A in P_D of sum_{T in A} 2^{-|union_{i in T} S_i|}. We conjecture that for every Boolean f with correlation eps with parities of degree <=a, the maximum linear bias of NW_{D,f} satisfies bias(NW_{D,f}) <= W(D) * eps, and moreover W(D) = Theta(m * 2^{-a}) on standard Galois-field NW designs, giving a tight Sperner-style replacement for the union-bound m * 2^{-a} factor in Nisan-Wigderson.

**Rationale (proposer's reasoning)**:

> The classical NW analysis pays a union-bound factor of m for the worst parity distinguisher, but the actual bias is a sum of Walsh coefficients indexed by subsets T of [m] whose contributions cancel along chains in the refinement poset; bounding cancellation by an LYM-type antichain width replaces the loose m factor with a combinatorially tight quantity. If the conjecture holds, NW seed length can shrink whenever W(D) << m, exposing a Sperner obstruction to natural-proofs-style attacks and giving a quantitative explanation for empirically observed sub-union-bound behavior.
