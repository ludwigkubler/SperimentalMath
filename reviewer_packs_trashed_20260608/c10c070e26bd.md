**Statement**:

> For a 3-regular graph G on n ≥ 8 vertices, let L̂_G = (D−A)/3 be the normalized Laplacian, μ_G its empirical spectral measure, and let κ_j(G) denote its jth free cumulant computed by Möbius inversion on NC(j) (κ_2 = m_2 − m_1², κ_4 = m_4 − 4 m_3 m_1 − 2 m_2² + 10 m_2 m_1² − 5 m_1⁴). Define the free-Poisson excess ε(G) := max(0, κ_4(G)/κ_2(G)² − 1) and the gap g(G) := SDP_2(G)/MaxCut(G), where SDP_2(G) = (3n/8)·(1 + λ_max(L̂_G − I)) is the Delorme–Poljak spectral SoS-2 upper bound. CONJECTURE: for every 3-regular G, (g(G) − 1) · log_2(n) ≤ 4 · (ε(G) + 1/n). A single instance violating this bound, or any instance with g(G) > 1 + 4·(ε(G)+1/n)/log_2 n, falsifies the conjecture.

**Rationale (proposer's reasoning)**:

> Free cumulants linearize free convolution and detect deviation from a Marchenko–Pastur / free-Poisson reference, so ε(G) measures exactly the bulk-spectrum 'non-randomness' that drives Delorme–Poljak slack on sparse random Cayley-like graphs. Because SDP_2 is a strict function of the Laplacian spectrum while MaxCut depends on combinatorial cycle structure, any controlled bound on (SDP_2/MaxCut − 1) by a spectral free-probability quantity offers a CSP-instance witness rather than a truth-table predicate, avoiding the NATURAL_PROOFS trap that killed the analogous Lasserre moment-rank attempt.
