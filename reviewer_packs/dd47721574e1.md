## 1. Conjecture
**Field A** (mathematical branch): Geometric group theory: equidistribution and mixing of Cayley walks on the finite simple group A_5
**Field B** (complexity object): Width-5 permutation branching programs (Barrington construction) for random 3-CNF formulas

**Statement**:

> Fix generators a = (1 2 3 4 5) and b = (1 3 5 2 4) of A_5. For a 3-CNF F with n variables and m clauses, define the Barrington walk W(F; x) in A_5 by, for each clause C_i = (l_{i,1} v l_{i,2} v l_{i,3}) and each literal position j in {0,1,2}, multiplying on the right by g(l_{i,j}, x) where g = a if (i+j) is even and the literal is satisfied by x, g = a^{-1} if (i+j) is even and the literal is falsified, g = b (resp. b^{-1}) if (i+j) is odd and the literal is satisfied (resp. falsified). Let mu_F(g) = Pr_{x ~ U({0,1}^n)}[W(F; x) = g] and define the equidistribution defect delta(F) = (1/2) * sum_{g in A_5} |mu_F(g) - 1/60|. Conjecture: there exists a universal alpha_* in [4.0, 4.5] such that for uniformly random 3-CNF F with m = round(alpha*n), (i) for alpha <= 3.5, E[delta(F)] = O(n^{-1/2}); (ii) for alpha >= 5.0, E[delta(F)] >= 0.10 uniformly in n; moreover for every individual F whose minimum resolution refutation has width > n/3, delta(F) >= c0 > 0 for an absolute constant c0.

**Rationale (proposer's reasoning)**:

> A_5 is the smallest non-solvable group and is the algebraic anchor of Barrington's theorem; geometric-group invariants of walks on its Cayley graph (mixing, equidistribution) are genuinely non-commutative measures that do not pull back to polynomial extensions of the Boolean ring, sidestepping algebrization. Heuristically, a low-density (satisfiable) formula has many cancelling assignments, letting the walk equidistribute on the simple group; high-density (rigid, UNSAT) formulas force commutator obstructions that pin the walk distribution away from uniform. This bridges a geometric-group-theoretic mixing invariant with the combinatorial structure of random 3-SAT, a linkage with essentially no prior literature.
