## 1. Conjecture
**Field A** (mathematical branch): Tropical Riemann-Roch: Baker-Norine divisor theory and chip-firing on graphs
**Field B** (complexity object): Resolution width and DPLL tree size of Tseitin formulas on 3-regular expanders

**Statement**:

> Let G be a connected 3-regular graph on n vertices with cyclomatic genus g(G) = |E| - |V| + 1, and let c: V(G) -> {0,1} be a 0/1 vertex charge with sum_v c(v) odd, defining the unsatisfiable Tseitin CNF Tseitin(G,c). View c as an effective divisor of degree |c| on the combinatorial graph G and compute its Baker-Norine rank r_BN(c) via Dhar's burning / q-reduction algorithm; let D(G,c) := g(G) - r_BN(c) be the rank gap. Conjecture: for every such (G, c), the fixed-order DPLL backtrack tree of Tseitin(G,c) has at least 2^{D(G,c)/3} leaves, and the maximum clause width touched in that DPLL run lies in the interval [D(G,c)/3, 3 D(G,c) + 5].

**Rationale (proposer's reasoning)**:

> Tseitin's parity obstruction is exactly the divisor-class obstruction [c] in the Picard group of G under chip-firing equivalence, and Baker-Norine rank quantifies how 'movable' c is across the graph — the same combinatorial degree of freedom that resolution must traverse to derive the global parity contradiction. Tropical Riemann-Roch on graphs has been entirely absent from proof complexity, yet it provides a sharp non-spectral, non-zeta handle: the genus-minus-rank gap is graph-theoretically invariant under chip-firing moves and is conceptually orthogonal to Ihara-zeta/Laplacian eigenvalue invariants. If true, the conjecture explains existing BSW Tseitin-on-expander width lower bounds via the fact that for 0/1 divisors of degree ~ n/2 the Baker-Norine rank generically saturates near -1, forcing D(G,c) ~ n/2.
